1

Prepare the variables

The datacube arrives with its variables already built to the data structure. The only preparation left is splitting one that should behave differently in different regions, or in different periods.

What happens

  • Nothing is built. Media, trade and sales columns arrive as variables; the KPI, distribution, price and category columns arrive with them.
  • Split by region when the same activity should act differently across regions: a sub-brand's TV is hero in its own region and halo in the others.
  • Split by period when the activity changed character: OOH bought differently in 2025, or a MAT 1 / MAT 2 cut.
  • The test of a good split: the pieces add back to the original, every week and every region. A split is a re-labelling, not new data.
  • Split only with a reason you can name. "The fit improved" is not one; cross-validation decides that in phase 6.
  • Pieces of one parent share a transform group, so the same channel does not get a different carryover per period.

2

Check the raw data

Codebase 1 saw variables that were already transformed, so problems in the raw activity were invisible. Now the model learns the transforms from untransformed activity, and any split we make can break, so both are checked before the fit.

What happens

  • A · Split checks: the pieces add back to their parent; units are preserved; periods neither overlap nor leave a gap.
  • B · Raw data checks: the KPI varies; spend matches the metric; cost-per-unit outliers (Meridian's rule); dust columns; variables with fewer than 5 active weeks.
  • New in Phase 2: can the data teach the transforms? Carryover needs weeks off air. Saturation needs weeks well above a typical week.
  • C · Design checks, before fitting: correlation; VIF, centred and uncentred; condition number; variable ≈ week or sub-brand; rows per parameter.
  • Grades: ERROR stops the run; ATTENTION goes to 00_warnings; INFO is only reported.

3

Prepare

Put every column on the axis its prior lives on, and give every learned parameter a range written in business units.

What happens

  • Scale with codebase 1's rule, using training weeks only:
    • KPI: per sub-brand, mean and sd;
    • media: ÷ the median active week, so 1.0 = a typical week and zero stays zero;
    • level drivers such as TDP and price: centred.
  • Lag tensor: for every week, the scaled media 0 … max_lag weeks earlier.
  • Bounds in business units:
    • half-life in weeks → decay;
    • EC50 in GRPs → typical weeks;
    • peak lag in weeks.
    A pin (fix_*) makes a parameter a constant.
  • The window caps the decay: max_lag = ceil(ln 0.05 ÷ ln α_max − 1). An 8-week window supports decay only up to 0.717.
  • min_lag is the fixed delay applied in preprocessing today. It is chosen by cross-validation, because NUTS cannot learn an integer.
  • Priors: saturating channels get a contribution-volume prior; everything else keeps codebase 1's coefficient priors and ±% units.

4

Fit the model

One model over all sub-brands and weeks. The sampler learns the carryover, the saturation, the coefficients, the baseline and the noise together.

What happens

  • Media: lag window → adstock (decay α, optional peak θ) → Hill saturation (EC50, slope) → × β for each sub-brand.
  • Shared vs per sub-brand: decay, EC50 and slope are learned once per channel, or once per channel family (a transform group). Only β varies by sub-brand, shrunk toward a shared mean.
  • Saturating channels: the prior is a contribution volume V. On every draw, the model sets β so the channel contributes exactly V, because the transformed media changes from draw to draw.
  • Everything else: codebase 1's buckets. Signs are structural (±exp); pooling is hierarchical, independent or global.

This is the algebra Meridian uses to turn an ROI prior into a coefficient, with units sold in place of money.

5

Diagnose

Did the sampler converge, did the data move the priors, and did learning the transforms leave the rest of the model readable?

What happens

  • Convergence, at codebase 1's standard: R-hat ≤ 1.01, divergences, effective sample size.
  • Did the data speak? Contraction for every coefficient, decay, EC50 and log volume.
  • New transform flags:
    • a parameter pressed against its bound;
    • an EC50 beyond anything observed;
    • decay trading off against β.
  • Residuals and structure: Durbin-Watson, heteroscedasticity, tails, the posterior predictive p-value on the total, and the probability the baseline goes negative.
  • Collinearity again, this time at the posterior-median transform.

6

Cross-validate and choose

One fit is weak evidence, especially once transforms are learned. Refit on growing windows and ask whether the forecasts, coefficients and transforms hold.

What happens

  • Scores per fold: wMAPE, CRPS and 90% predictive coverage on the test weeks.
  • Stability across folds: the coefficients, and the learned decay and EC50. A decay that moves when the cut-off moves has not been pinned down.
  • Choosing between specifications, with codebase 1's rule:
    1. pass the gate (R-hat, divergences, coverage);
    2. more accurate only if better by more than one standard error;
    3. more stable;
    4. fewer parameters.
  • This is where the builder's freedom gets tested. Two recipes are two specifications, compared like any others.

7

Report in units sold

Replay every posterior draw to get each driver's weekly contribution, check that the parts add up, and roll them up the way the business reads them.

What happens

  • Contributions in units sold, per sub-brand and week, with intervals. On every draw the parts add up exactly to fitted sales.
  • Baseline, expanded: core (intercept, trend, seasonality) + TDP + base price + category + dummies + competitor TV.
  • Incremental effects roll up by pillar: TV, Online Media, Trade.
  • Reconciliation chain: raw → scaled → contribution → fitted → actual, with each step in its own file.
  • Benchmark sheet: paste the vendor's volumes. They are already in the same units as our volume priors.

How the modelling differs from Meridian

The structure is the same: adstock, then Hill, coefficients pooled across regions, and all parameters sampled together by NUTS. The differences sit in scaling, priors, the baseline and validation.

Same inversion, different currency

Neither model samples the media coefficient directly. Each draw first computes the transformed media, then solves for the β that delivers a target. Meridian's target is ROI × spend; ours is a contribution volume.

A prior written directly on β would mean something different at every decay and EC50. A prior on volume means the same thing on every draw.

Flexible or parametric baseline

Meridian's per-week time effects absorb every week-to-week movement shared by all regions. Most of our media is national, identical in every sub-brand, so the number of knots would decide how much of that movement counts as baseline and how much as media.

A smooth trend + Fourier baseline leaves more of the movement to the drivers, but it extrapolates poorly: that is codebase 1's Q4 holdout problem. Knots remain an option (P3).

Same Hill, different window rule

Both normalise the carryover weights, so adstock is a weighted average of recent weeks and EC50 stays on the typical-week axis.

With Meridian's default 9-week window, any decay above about 0.72 already loses more than 5% of its carryover to the cut-off, yet the prior still allows decay up to 1. We state the cap, tie it to the window, and allow a learned peak and a fixed delay.

New in Phase 2

What neither codebase 1 nor Meridian provides today.

Variable splitting, checked

Split by region or period, with the pieces proven to add back to the parent and sharing one transform group.

Checks on raw data

Split checks, a unit guard, and "can this channel teach carryover and saturation?" before any sampling.

Lag window

A fixed min_lag, a learned peak θ, and a decay cap derived from max_lag.

Transform groups

Decay and EC50 shared within a channel family: 14 transform parameters instead of 59.

Bound diagnostics

Flags for a parameter pressed against its bound, an EC50 beyond the data, and decay trading off with β.

Volume priors inverted per draw

The vendor's volumes are used as priors directly, with no ROI and no hand division by Σx.

CV chooses the variables

Recipe variants and min_lag variants are compared with the same scorecard as any other specification.

Bridge test to codebase 1

Pin every transform at today's preprocessing values: codebase 2 should then reproduce codebase 1.
