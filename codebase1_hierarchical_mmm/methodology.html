At a glance

Short-term MMM explains how sales respond to activity within a 2-year window. LTE measures the slower effect of media on the brand's underlying base, and adds it to the short-term contribution to give total impact, long-term multipliers and ROI. The method's structure is sound. Three findings drive most of the proposed changes.

What we keep

  • The eight stages, from data stitching to media wave analysis.
  • Nesting the UCM trend into the AO MMM model, with media, expert and negative baseline locked.
  • The retrofit, which carries only the UCM to the other years.
  • The media wave attribution by calendar year.

What we change, most important first

  1. Fix the start-up fault in the UCM trend that understates Year 1.
  2. Define the trend smoothing in calendar time. λ = 300 means about 6 months on weekly data and 26 months on monthly data.
  3. Report the multiplier on a stated basis, indexed and non-indexed.
  4. Split media waves at the stock, so spill-over is not under-credited.
  5. Show which numbers come from the data and which come from priors. The UCM's share of sales (20.3% in FR PDX) scales every long-term number.

New: a validation layer

The current method has no formal checks between stages. We add one gate per stage, each with a pass mark agreed in advance. The first gate reproduces today's numbers exactly before anything changes. The others check the trend, placebo-test the long-term model, confirm the nested model reproduces the AO MMM model, test the retrofit in both directions, check that the waves add up, reconcile exactly and report sensitivity ranges.

To decide

  • Pre-period handling.
  • The smoothing cut-off, in months.
  • Which AO MMM model to nest into: the latest or the earlier 2 years.
  • Brand level or pools.
  • The multiplier basis.
  • The wave-split convention.

All twelve open questions are in section 7.

1The current methodology

The idea

  • Sales move for two reasons: short-term activation over weeks, and a slowly moving base over years. A 2-year MMM measures the first and puts the second in its baseline.
  • LTE extracts the slow base of sales as a statistical trend (the UCM trend), explains that trend with long-memory media (the UCM model), and measures how much of sales the trend accounts for by adding it to the 2-year MMM as one more variable (nesting).
  • The long-term contribution of a medium is its share of the trend multiplied by the trend's share of sales.
  • Results are reported for Years 1 and 2 of the history, and added to the short-term contribution already reported for those years.

2Proposed methodology

Principles

  • Keep the structure and vocabulary of the current method. Nothing here changes what LTE reports.
  • Reproduce today's numbers exactly first. Then change one thing at a time, and show each change's effect on one BMC side by side.
  • Fix what produces wrong numbers, improve what makes numbers fragile, and leave the rest optional.
  • Make every assumption-driven number visible, with a range.
Essential fixes a wrong number Recommended makes a number more robust Optional useful, not required Decision needs the team's choice

A. UCM extraction

A1

Fix the start-up fault in the UCM trend

Essential
Today
Sales are fitted in raw units with the library's default start-up setting.
Issue
The trend starts far below sales and takes weeks to catch up: 42% of sales in week 1 of the France output, and about six weeks to reach 90% ✓. Colombia starts at 52%, and the vendor's own example jumps from about 20K to 90K. The fault falls inside reported Year 1, roughly 5% of France's Year-1 trend, concentrated in the first quarter. The cause is a default start-up variance of 10⁶, tiny next to sales of 10⁵–10⁷.
Proposal
Scale sales before fitting and use exact ("diffuse") start-up initialisation. Keep the pre-period fill as a safeguard.
Benefit
A correct trend from the first week. Year 1 is no longer understated.
Cost
Low effort. Numbers move against past studies, so we show both side by side once. FR PDX's own trend chart does not show the fault, so that study may have corrected it by hand; the material does not say.
A2

Give the model dates, and keep the cycle only when it matters

Essential
Today
No dates are given to the model, and the cycle is always included.
Issue
The "cycle" is not a business cycle. In the brand-level series it is a few thousandths of a percent of sales (France; Colombia's total), and its length is unconstrained: about 3 weeks in the France output.
Proposal
Give the model dates, so cycles are bounded to 1.5–12 years. Keep the cycle only if it is material (more than 0.5% of the level) or improves the fit, and report it.
Benefit
Interpretable components and a cleaner trend.
Cost
Low. A 4-year series cannot identify a business cycle anyway, so the cycle will usually drop out.
A3

Set the trend smoothing in calendar time

EssentialDecision
Today
HP λ = 300 for every cadence.
Issue
λ = 300 removes swings shorter than about 6 months on weekly data, but about 26 months on monthly data ✓. "Long-term" then means different things by BMC. Keeping the 6-month meaning on monthly data needs λ ≈ 1.
Proposal
Agree the cut-off in months and derive λ for each cadence. Report the sensitivity at half and double the cut-off.
Benefit
The same meaning across BMCs, written down.
Cost
A decision. Long-term shares move with the cut-off, and we show that as a range.

A4

Test for seasonality

Recommended
Today
The method says to test for seasonality; the notebooks never do.
Issue
Seasonality left in the trend shows up as "mid-term fluctuation" that the HP filter then has to remove.
Proposal
Test automatically (ACF and a fit comparison). Include a seasonal component when it improves the fit, and report the decision.
Benefit
A cleaner trend, with less reliance on the HP filter.
Cost
Weekly seasonality on 4 years is weakly identified, so keep it simple, with few harmonics.
A5

Test that sales have a persistent component

Recommended
Today
Not tested.
Issue
Reading long-term effects off a trend assumes sales have a persistent component. Without one, the "trend" is smoothed noise.
Proposal
Test it: trend variance above zero, a comparison with a straight line, and standard unit-root tests. Flag BMCs that fail.
Benefit
Stops long-term claims on series that cannot support them.
Cost
Low.
A6

Stabilise the end of the trend

Recommended
Today
The HP filter runs on the full sample.
Issue
The filter is least reliable at the end of the data, which is where the latest nested window sits. Each refresh revises last year's trend.
Proposal
Extend the series by a short forecast before smoothing and trim it after. Report how far the trend moved at each refresh.
Benefit
A more stable UCM in the latest window, with revisions visible.
Cost
Moderate.
A7

Use a back-cast only to warm up the trend

Essential
Today
With short history, 6 months are back-cast with a time-reversed seasonal forecast.
Issue
Sales can be back-cast; media cannot. If back-cast months enter a model, media effects are biased towards zero.
Proposal
Use back-cast months only to warm up the trend, and drop them before any model.
Benefit
No invented data in any regression.
Cost
None.

B. UCM modelling

B1

Show which long-term shares the data supports

Essential
Today
Fit statistics are reported (FR PDX: R² 98.5%, MAPE 1.0%).
Issue
One smooth series regressed on other smooth series always fits well, so the fit does not validate the shares. The share priors (baseline 70–80%, TV 20–25%) do much of the work.
Proposal
For every coefficient, report whether the data moved it away from its prior. Run a placebo (media shifted in time) and a baseline-only model. Label the shares the data did not move.
Benefit
Readers can tell evidence from assumption.
Cost
Two extra small fits.
B2

Extract the trend from base sales as a sensitivity

Optional
Today
The trend is taken from total sales.
Issue
Long flights or promotions can leak into the trend and be credited to long-term media, which double counts them.
Proposal
Where a full-period short-term decomposition exists, also extract the trend from sales minus short-term media and trade.
Benefit
Less double counting.
Cost
Needs that decomposition, and differs from the vendor's trend.

C. Nested modelling

C1

Control and declare the UCM's share of sales

Essential
Today
The UCM prior is iterated "after reviewing the results".
Issue
The UCM is a smoothed piece of sales itself, so it can absorb almost anything. Its share (FR PDX 20.3%) is driven by its prior and by which variables may give up volume. Repeated prior corrections do not converge; we have seen this on a previous project.
Proposal
Keep the locks on media, expert and negative baseline. Allow one prior correction at most. Re-run with the UCM prior at 0.5×, 1× and 1.5× and report the range. Audit where the UCM's volume came from, which should be the positive baseline only. Label the UCM share as assumption-driven when the data does not move it.
Benefit
The one number that scales every long-term result becomes transparent and bounded.
Cost
Two or three extra nested fits.

D. Retrofit

D1

Test the retrofit assumption

Recommended
Today
The scalar from one window is applied to the other, assuming no step change.
Issue
The retrofit's key assumption is never checked, and the retrofitted UCM sits in a decomposition that never contained it.
Proposal
Flag any week where the UCM would exceed the positive baseline, and any jump at the boundary. Where AO MMM models cover both 2-year windows, nest into both and compare the two scalars. The retrofit can run either way, so this is a direct test.
Benefit
The assumption is tested rather than assumed.
Cost
One extra nested fit where two models exist.

E. Media waves

E1

Split media waves at the stock, not at the saturated response

RecommendedDecision
Today
Each year's support goes through the whole chain, saturation (Hill) included, and each week is split in the ratio of the results.
Issue
Hill is nonlinear, so the split depends on the curve's shape. A carried-over stock holding 14.3% of the total gets 23.4% of the credit at S = 1, 6.1% at S = 2 and 1.2% at S = 3 ✓. The method expects S above 1 for long-term media, so spill-over, which is what waves measure, is under-credited. The deck's worked example has no Hill, so it does not show this.
Proposal
Split the linear steps (SES, adstock, lag) exactly by year. At the Hill step, give each year its share of the stock.
Benefit
Exact, adds up, and independent of the curve. Identical to today when there is no Hill.
Cost
Needs each year's pre-Hill stock from the transformation step. Spill-over shares rise for S-shaped channels, so run both methods side by side first.
E2

Project the wave tail beyond the data

Optional
Today
Years 1–2 are reported, and the effect beyond the data is ignored.
Issue
Part of a wave's effect lies after the last date; the method's own example puts it at 13% for Year 3 and 47% for Year 4. With 3–3.5 years of data even Year 2 is truncated, without a flag.
Proposal
Project each wave's remaining effect with no further activity, and report the share that is unobserved.
Benefit
An honest Year-2 wave.
Cost
Assumes the scalar and coefficients hold after the data.

F. Reporting

F1

Report the multiplier on a stated basis

Essential
Today
One multiplier is reported, with the basis unstated.
Issue
The method says long-term contributions are "indexed", that is, re-booked to the year of the activity. FR PDX's waves are indexed (TV about 2.4 ✓), but its ROI slide is not: TV 2.03, which matches the long-term effect realised within 2022–23 (about 2.1 reconstructed ✓).
Proposal
Report both, labelled. Indexed shows what the year's money bought, the investment view. Non-indexed shows how much of the year's sales is long-term, the calendar view.
Benefit
No ambiguity in the headline multiplier.
Cost
None.
F2

Show ranges, not only points

Recommended
Today
Point estimates only.
Issue
Nothing shows how firm a number is.
Proposal
Show the range from the sensitivity runs (cut-off ½× and 2×, seasonal on or off, UCM prior 0.5×–1.5×) beside every headline number.
Benefit
Decision-makers see how firm each number is.
Cost
Compute only.
F3

Keep the UCM at brand level

Decision
Today
The method works at brand level, but one notebook runs the UCM per pool.
Issue
Pool-level trends do not add up to the brand's: a 4% gap ✓.
Proposal
Brand level by default. In a pooled nested model, enter the brand trend in every pool and sum the results.
Benefit
Adds up by construction.
Cost
None.

5Contribution calculations and reconciliation

The chain

  1. UCM model. Each lever's contribution to the trend each week (c), and the trend itself (τ).
  2. Share of the trend. p = c ÷ τ each week. Over a period, Σc ÷ Στ: a ratio of sums, not an average of weekly shares.
  3. Nested model. The UCM's contribution to sales in the nested window, U.
  4. Scalar. κ = U ÷ τ, constant over the nested window. In FR PDX the UCM is 20.3% of sales; the deck's example scalar is 19% of the trendline.
  5. Retrofit. U = κ × τ in every week of the other years. The positive baseline factors of the reported decomposition are reduced proportionally, so the reported total is unchanged. Nothing else is touched.
  6. Long-term contribution of lever i: L = p × U = κ × c, every week, in both windows.
  7. Final decomposition. Lever i = its short-term contribution + L. Baseline = the short-term baseline after the UCM's carve-out, plus the UCM model's baseline share of U. The UCM row disappears and the total equals actual sales.
  8. Waves. Each week's L is split by year of activity. Summed per year, it gives the indexed long-term contribution.
  9. Multipliers. (short + long term) ÷ short term, on both bases. ROI = value ÷ spend, optional, and needs a price.

Reconciliation checks, all exact

  1. The nested model's components add to fitted sales, and with the residual to actual sales.
  2. The scalar κ is the same in every week of the nested window.
  3. The UCM model's contributions add to the trend.
  4. The long-term contributions across levers, plus the long-term baseline, equal the UCM contribution in every week.
  5. In retrofitted years the reported total is unchanged; only the positive baseline is reduced, by exactly the UCM's contribution.
  6. The final decomposition equals actual sales in every week.
  7. The wave splits add up to each week's long-term contribution, and each wave's table sums to 100%.
  8. Percentages sum to 100% in every period.

Two arithmetics, kept apart. Weekly tables are sums of weekly central estimates, so they add up exactly. Uncertainty intervals come from the full posterior and are reported separately; they do not add, by design.

SES       e_t = α·a_t + (1 − α)·e_(t−1)        α = 0.05; half-life ≈ 13.5 periods  ✓

Adstock   m_t = h_t + λ_a·m_(t−1)               half-life = ln 0.5 / ln λ_a
order     SES → Hill → adstock (deck), to be confirmed (Q5)

τ_t = intercept + slope·t + Σ_i b_i·m_(t,i) + drivers + media residual + e_t
c_(t,i) = b_i·m_(t,i)
share of the trend over a period P:   Σ_(t∈P) c_(t,i)  ÷  Σ_(t∈P) τ_t

Glossary

UCM trend
The slow base of sales: an unobserved-components model's level plus growth, smoothed.
HP filter, λ
A smoother; λ sets the shortest swing it keeps.
SES, α
Single exponential smoothing: a long-memory adstock whose weights sum to 1.
Hill, K and S
The saturation curve. K is the half-saturation point and S the steepness; S above 1 gives an S-shaped curve.
UCM model
The long-term model: the UCM trend regressed on long-term media.
AO MMM model
The team's 2-year short-term sales MMM.
Nesting
Adding the UCM trend to the AO MMM model as one more baseline variable.
Positive and negative baseline
Baseline drivers with positive effects (distribution, category) or negative effects (competitor media, price).
Donor
A variable allowed to give up volume when the UCM is added.
Scalar κ
The UCM's contribution divided by the trendline. It carries the UCM to the other years.
Retrofit
Carrying the UCM contribution, and only it, to the years outside the nested window.
Media wave
One calendar year of a channel's activity, followed into later years.
Indexed contribution
Long-term effect booked back to the year of the activity that caused it.
Non-indexed contribution
Long-term effect booked to the week it is realised.
Multiplier
(Short-term + long-term) ÷ short-term.
Contraction
How far the data moved a coefficient away from its prior. Near zero means the number is the prior.
Placebo test
Refitting with media shifted in time. A real effect should beat it.
